## Is there a published taxonomy of mobile game store screenshot types or best-practice ordering?

### Takeaway
No single, formally-named taxonomy of screenshot *types* (e.g., "gameplay / key art / progression / character-collection / social / events") was found published by Apple, Google, SplitMetrics, or StoreMaven. What exists instead is (a) official platform rules about screenshot count/composition, (b) a strong industry consensus heuristic that the first screenshot(s) must carry the core value proposition since most users don't scroll past screenshot 3, and (c) GameRefinery's mechanics/feature-based taxonomy (52 subgenres, 260 features) which is the closest thing to a structured classification system, though it is not literally a "screenshot type" taxonomy.

### Cited Findings
- Apple: "App Store required screenshots are the first three portrait screenshots or one landscape screenshot from the app page"; up to 10 screenshots per device type; at least one is required for submission; promotional text overlays are not allowed and screenshots "must show real app UI, not just marketing banners" — [AppFollow: App Store and Google Play Screenshot Guidelines](https://appfollow.io/blog/app-store-and-google-play-screenshot-guidelines)
- Google Play: minimum of two screenshots required, up to 8 per device type; promotional text in screenshots is permitted "with limitations" — [AppFollow: App Store and Google Play Screenshot Guidelines](https://appfollow.io/blog/app-store-and-google-play-screenshot-guidelines)
- Widely repeated industry stat: "About 90% of users don't scroll past the third screenshot" — cited across multiple ASO best-practice articles as the reason the first three screenshots must carry the core value proposition, unique selling points, and key visuals/characters — [multiple ASO blog sources, e.g. AppFollow guidelines roundup]
- SplitMetrics guidance (via Medium/blog materials): "Start your screenshot sequence with a key message that powerfully summarizes your app's main value proposition"; recommends full localization of screenshot text, imagery, and even feature *order* per market, with re-testing per locale — [SplitMetrics: Ultimate Guide to App Store Screenshots](https://medium.com/splitmetrics/the-ultimate-guide-to-app-store-screenshots-sizes-styles-tips-and-best-practices-4833b5472369)
- SplitMetrics and StoreMaven are positioned industry-wide as A/B-*testing* platforms for screenshots ("test one variable at a time and let results, not opinion, decide") rather than publishers of a fixed content-type taxonomy — [strataigize.com: SplitMetrics vs StoreMaven comparison](https://www.strataigize.com/blog/splitmetrics-vs-storemaven-2025)
- A mobile-game-specific ASO blog (ASOMobile) recommends organizing a game's screenshot set around genre-appropriate visual aesthetics, sequential/story-like progression across the set, prioritizing the "most exciting and vibrant content," and integrating captions/CTAs — but explicitly frames ordering as something to A/B test rather than a fixed prescribed sequence: "visual elements must be tested regularly. A/B tests help identify the optimal icons, screenshot order, text, and video style." It also warns against exaggerated/misleading gameplay footage — [ASOMobile: Visual ASO of Mobile Games 2025](https://asomobile.net/en/blog/visual-aso-of-mobile-games-2025/)
- GameRefinery's taxonomy (feature-based, not screenshot-type-based): 260 manually-tracked features from "core" to "meta" to "individual ad features," rolled up into 52 subgenres by combining core mechanic + meta features (example given: match-3 core + character-development meta = "Puzzle RPG" subgenre) — [GameRefinery: New Genre Taxonomy and Why We Need It](https://www.gamerefinery.com/new-genre-taxonomy-and-why-we-need-it/), [Genre Taxonomy Update April 2021](https://www.gamerefinery.com/genre-taxonomy-update-april-2021/)

### Inferences
- The absence of a named, universally-cited "screenshot type taxonomy" (hero/gameplay/character/social/progression/events, etc.) from official or major ASO-vendor sources suggests that any such taxonomy used in a competitor-analysis tool (like Stora's "hero value prop / social proof / feature showcase" categories) is a proprietary/ad-hoc classification scheme built by that tool's authors, not an adopted industry standard — worth designing your own taxonomy rather than searching for a canonical one to adopt wholesale.
- GameRefinery's mechanics-first taxonomy is a better fit for classifying *what gameplay is shown* in a screenshot (useful for games specifically) than for classifying the *marketing role* a screenshot plays (which is closer to what Stora's pattern labels and the objective's "gameplay / key art / progression / character / social / events" list are trying to capture) — these are two different, complementary axes worth keeping separate in any tool design.
