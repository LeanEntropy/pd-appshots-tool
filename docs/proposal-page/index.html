Internal tool proposal · v2 (revised)

Store Screenshot Recipe

Pulls competitor Google Play screenshots, analyses them in an expanding circle of checks, and recommends exactly which screen to capture and how to design each slot of our own store listing — showing you the image count and cost before every step.

Revised plan: awaiting your review
What changed since the first version: the pipeline now grows outward from a small, capped core instead of analysing everything at once — an expanding circle that starts with 5–8 close competitors in English, checks itself for a stable pattern, then lets you choose what to add next. The core check is hard-capped at 80–100 AI-analysed images, and every step, core or expansion, shows the exact image count before it runs. Screenshots are now sorted into cost tiers — identical, lightly re-checked, or fully analysed — so only genuinely new content gets a full read. The models changed to a performance-first line-up (Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, gpt-5.6-sol, with GPT-6 Astra reserved for special cases; no Sonnet, no Haiku). And every step is named in plain language throughout — "reading each screenshot," "checking the analysis," never a step code.

Your decisions, first

Six open decisions (items 38–43 of the revised proposal). Each has a recommended default, highlighted below — approve it, ask for a change, or flag a question.

38

Which competitors form the core check, and in what priority order?

RecommendedCapybara Go, Mini Tales, Heroll, Dicero, Archero 2, Survivor.io, BagMaster Isekai — 7 games, in that order.

AlternativesSwap in Legend of Mushroom, Legend of Slime or Overgeared Hero in place of one of the seven.

WhyThis mixes the direct competitors (not yet very successful, but the closest match) with same-audience games, while staying inside the whole-deck fit rule for the image cap.

39

Where exactly should the core cap sit, within the 80–100 range?

Recommended100 as the hard stop, aiming for about 85 images in practice.

AlternativesSet the hard stop lower, e.g. 90, to save cost · skip an aiming point and just enforce the 100 hard stop.

Why85 comfortably fits our own 7 images plus 7–8 whole competitor decks at roughly 9.5 images each, while the 100 hard stop leaves headroom for a slightly longer deck without refusing to start.

40

When should the core check suggest stopping early?

RecommendedAfter at least 5 competitor decks, once the last 2 decks changed no slot's top screen type and no share moved more than 10 points.

AlternativesRequire more decks (e.g. 7) before allowing an early stop · use a tighter or looser share-shift threshold.

Why5 decks is enough to see a real pattern without spending the whole cap on it; requiring 2 unchanged decks in a row guards against stopping on a fluke.

41

How much of the AI output gets a second, cross-family read?

Recommended100% in the core check; in expansions, every full analysis plus 25% of the layer-difference checks and anything low-confidence.

AlternativesReview 100% everywhere, for maximum confidence at higher cost · sample less than 25% in expansions to save further.

WhyThe core check sets the pattern for everything after it, so it's worth reviewing in full; once the process is validated, expansions can sample.

42

What's the budget ceiling per expansion?

Recommended$30, adjustable in config.

AlternativesNo fixed ceiling · a different default value.

WhyA per-increment ceiling keeps each expansion's cost predictable and bounded, while staying a config value you can raise.

43

Which library reads our own PSD's layers and text?

Recommendedpsd-tools (Python) — already proven on our PSD via the spike script.

Alternativesag-psd (TypeScript-only, avoids a second runtime, but unproven on our PSD).

WhyA proven reader beats an unproven one, even at the cost of adding a second runtime to the repo.

The proposal

Items 1–29, grouped as in the revised proposal. Approve each one, or flag a change or question, with an optional note.

A. What the tool is

1

A reusable, config-driven repo

Operated with Claude Code. For each locale it recommends the screenshot order and count, the in-game screen to capture, a full marketing-layer spec, translation notes, and what to change in our current images.

2

Code first, AI only with your OK

Code does everything that doesn't need judgement. AI is used only where judgement is needed, and never without your approval of the image count and cost first.

3

Rogue Legends is the first config

The tool is game-agnostic. Other games need a new config file, not code changes.

B. How it works: the expanding circle

4

Setup, at no AI cost

First shows how many decks and images it will download, and the disk space, and waits for your OK. Then downloads our deck, the core competitors' English decks, and the top-10 grossing decks for every configured country, and shows them all raw.

5

Core check

  • Our deck plus 5–8 of the closest named competitors, US English only.
  • Capped at 80–100 AI-analysed images in total, with a hard stop.
  • Whole decks only: the tool picks as many complete competitor decks as fit, in your priority order, shows the exact count, and won't start if fewer than 5 fit.
  • Only our live store screenshots (7) count; our local files are matched to them and kept as reference.
  • It starts with a small pilot: ours plus 2 competitors.
6

Early stop

When the pattern stops changing, the tool suggests stopping. You decide.

7

First recommendation

For US English. You rate it in the report page.

8

The tool then asks how to expand

  • More English competitors.
  • Countries, from a checkbox list.
  • Chart games, picked from the raw view.

You can pick more than one.

9

Each expansion is a separate increment

Before it runs, the tool shows: how many new images it will process, split by cost level; how many model passes that means, including the review model (so 85 images reads as about 170 passes); anything it must add first, e.g. the English analysis of a game you picked for a country; and the estimated cost. It waits for your OK. When a budget ceiling is hit, the lowest-priority competitor/country pairs are dropped first.

C. How it keeps cost down (cheapest first)

10

Identical to the English image: no AI

The analysis is reused and the screenshot is flagged "unchanged from English" — about the screenshot, not the whole store page.

11

Same screen with a new marketing layer

Only the cropped band and text areas are sent, plus the English analysis, and a lighter capable model returns the differences.

12

Different screenshot

Full analysis with the strongest model.

13

Cropping and batching

Images are cropped and resized to what the model needs. A whole deck is analysed per call where possible. Translation works on text only.

D. Models (performance first)

14

Reading each screenshot: Fable 5.1

It was best at reading band colours in our test.

15

Marketing-layer differences: Opus 5.5

It read every Asian-script headline correctly in our test. This is a smaller job than a full read: it compares the cropped text and band areas against the English analysis.

16

Checking the image analysis: gpt-5.6-sol

The most accurate GPT model in our test. GPT-6 Astra is kept for special cases only and never used for document reviews.

17

Settling disagreements, translation and recommendations: Opus 5.5

Covers adjudication, translation and genre lingo, and per-country recommendations.

18

Checking the recommendations: gpt-5.6-sol

To be confirmed in the pilot.

19

No Sonnet, no Haiku

No Sonnet is used for screenshots and no Haiku is used anywhere.

E. What you get

20

Local folder = source of truth

Holds all images (full-res and optimised), all analysis data, and offline markdown reports: per locale, per competitor deck, our assets, recommendations, and a chart-games gallery. Every run is dated and can be compared with earlier runs.

21

A private report page (claude.ai Artifact)

Built from the same data. Shows recommendations (current vs. recommended side by side), every analysed deck in store order, a locale comparison, the raw chart games, and statistics. If Artifacts aren't available, the same page is built as an offline folder.

22

Feedback

Approve / reject / note on every recommendation and screenshot, plus comments. The next run applies these as tracked corrections.

23

Screenshot count is a finding, not a setting

The tool reports how many screenshots competitors use per locale, and recommends a count capped at 8, framed as "what competitors do." Google Play allows 8 phone screenshots; longer competitor decks likely mix device types, and are still analysed in full.

F. Milestones (each shows its image count and waits for you)

24

Setup

Repo, config, taxonomy, a host check, and all downloads. You: approve the download preview, then confirm the core competitor list and its exact image count.

25

Core pilot

About 25 images, 100% cross-checked. Also tests the "same screen, new layer" check on hand-labelled pairs (no AI cost), and measures real costs. You: rate the accuracy; the taxonomy is then frozen.

26

Core check

About 55–65 more images (about 80–90 in total), plus the first US-English recommendation. You: rate it and choose expansions.

27

Expansions

Repeatable, each approved separately. You: approve each one and rate its recommendation.

28

Final report and feedback loop

The final report is published and feedback is collected for the next run.

29

Estimated cost

Core check about $17–28 on your Claude subscription plus about 1.5–1.8M gpt-5.6-sol tokens (provisional; recomputed from the pilot's real numbers). Each added country about $4–6 (the real counts are shown before approval).

GPT cross-review (docs/plan-v2.review-gpt.md, reviewer gpt-5.6-sol via Codex CLI) returned 14 findings against the revised plan and proposal. All 14 were accepted or applied, one only partly; two of them (the non-existent gpt-6-sol slug, and the recommendation-reviewer default) were already resolved by the coordinator's model-name correction — Sol as the default GPT reviewer — made right after the review ran.

Real fixes the review drove: the core check now has an explicit whole-deck fit rule, so it always tries for at least 5 complete competitor decks and refuses to start rather than risk stopping mid-deck; the "8 screenshots" figure is now framed as our own recommendation cap, not a Play-imposed maximum, since real decks of 10 and 13 exist; setup shows a download preview and waits for approval before pulling any images at all; approval screens now show both the unique images counted against the cap and the separate model-pass count (so 85 images reading as ~170 passes is visible, not hidden); expansion planning bundles in any missing English baseline analysis automatically; and settled items like the 80–100 cap and whole-deck calls moved out of the open-decisions list into "Already decided" above, rather than being re-litigated as if still open.
